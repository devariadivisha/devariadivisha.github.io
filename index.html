What connects it all

A summer school in the Netherlands during my bachelor's — studying Randstad's sustainable urbanism — reoriented how I saw infrastructure. I came back to India building roads for motors while they were designing cities for people. That gap has driven everything since: the edX courses on circular economy and inclusive cities, the TCFD certification, the IIT Kanpur degree, and the capstone project applying ML to infrastructure bid data.

The question I keep returning to: how do we make infrastructure decisions — about what to build, where, and at what cost — more rigorous, more data-driven, and more aligned with long-term sustainability?
Currently based in Abu Dhabi, UAE. Open to roles in infrastructure strategy, proposals/BD, data analytics, and ESG-linked infrastructure advisory. Also open to PhD and Masters programmes in infrastructure analytics, sustainable urbanism, or built environment data science — with preference for UAE-based institutions and shortlisted international programmes.

Research interests

My work keeps returning to one underlying question: how do the decisions that shape infrastructure — what to build, where, at what cost, and for whom — get made, and how could they be made better? My bachelor's thesis examined why PPP highway contracts fail financially in India. My IIT Kanpur capstone used ML to predict competitive bid outcomes. The tool I built extends that into a decision-support system that is now being used on live bids.

The research questions I would want to pursue further sit at three intersections: infrastructure procurement and financial risk (why do cost and time overruns persist despite decades of research?), data-driven decision-making in capital-intensive environments (how do we build tools that practitioners actually trust and use?), and sustainable infrastructure finance (how does climate risk get priced into long-horizon infrastructure assets, particularly in the GCC?).

Featured — Bid Intelligence Tool

Strategic Bid Intelligence Tool — v6 Built · Python · Streamlit
Extended from IIT Kanpur Capstone · 2025 · RIPL Internal

A production-ready Go/No-Go decision engine built on RIPL's own historical bid data. The tool automates what previously required days of manual work — from document extraction to market benchmarking to final verdict — in a single session.

What it does

Upload a tender PDF (NIT, Schedule B, or RFP). The tool auto-extracts 19+ fields, runs a Ridge Regression model trained on 204 awarded projects, calculates your cost position against market floor and ceiling, scores 5 strategic dimensions, and returns a deterministic GO / CONDITIONAL GO / NO-GO verdict with full reasoning.

What it knows

204 awarded projects across 5 sectors. Model R²: Highway 0.818, Railway 0.962. Covers sector competitiveness, state-wise bidding aggressiveness, year-on-year market trends, and RIPL's own win rate by segment (e.g. Ropeway HAM: 50% win rate, Highway EPC: 6%).
